`signed_input + (fast_trace - medium_trace)`. The bounded question was whether

## Observations / reduction

- `191/280` cells emitted negative polarity despite a positive probe.
- `89/280` emitted positive polarity; `0/280` were below the emission threshold.
- `sign(probe + derivative)` matched the emitted direction in `280/280` cells.
- Every tested ITI retained at least one inversion through an `80 ms` post-train
  gap; no inversion remained at `160 ms` in this bounded grid.
- Example: ITI `10 ms`, gap `20 ms`, probe `1.0` produced derivative
  `-1.0817059176`, so `probe + derivative = -0.0817059176` and polarity flipped.
- Example: ITI `5 ms`, gap `80 ms`, probe `0.1` produced derivative
  `-0.1282277256`, again sufficient to flip the sign.

The result is exactly reduced by the implementation rule. The current probe adds
the same signed increment to fast and medium traces, so their difference is
inherited from the pre-probe adaptation history. After a dense positive train,
the fast trace can decay below the slower medium trace; a sufficiently negative
derivative then reverses the emitted sign. No additional memory/adaptation
mechanism is needed.
